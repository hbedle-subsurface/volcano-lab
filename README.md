# Read the Volcano

An interactive volcano lab for introductory students, set in the Cascades with Hawaii for contrast.

I built this lab for my online gen ed course on volcanoes and earthquakes at the University of Oklahoma, where most students are not science majors. It is the companion to [Find the Earthquake](https://hbedle-subsurface.github.io/earthquake-lab/). Students learn by moving sliders and reading real data, and the lab checks their answers as they go.

**Open the lab:** https://hbedle-subsurface.github.io/volcano-lab/

## What students do

1. **Magma** - change silica and dissolved gas, and watch viscosity, rock name, eruption style and volcano shape change.
2. **Hotspot speed** - fit a line to the ages of the Hawaiian-Emperor volcanoes (Clague and Dalrymple, 1987) to find the speed of the Pacific plate, and contrast Hawaii with the Cascade volcanic arc.
3. **St. Helens wakes up** - step through March 15 to May 18, 1980 with a date slider and watch the monitoring signals (earthquakes, steam explosions, the bulge) from the USGS record.
4. **Hazards race** - race pyroclastic flows, lahars, lava flows, a runner and a car to a town, with Mount Rainier and Orting as the real example.
5. **How big? (VEI)** - use the Volcanic Explosivity Index (Newhall and Self, 1982) and a cube comparison to size eruptions against St. Helens.
6. **Your turn** - classify a mystery volcano from a data card: rock, eruption style, tectonic setting, VEI and main hazard. Each student gets one of six real volcanoes.

When all six steps are done, the lab makes a certificate with a verification code that students upload to the course site. Most students need about an hour to an hour and a half.

Words with a dotted underline open a short glossary entry. The interactive panel and the instructions can each be popped out into their own window.

## Data sources

- Hawaiian-Emperor ages and distances: Clague and Dalrymple (1987, 1989), USGS Professional Paper 1350, as tabulated by Seattle Central College (QELP) and NOAA Ocean Exploration.
- Mount St. Helens 1980 timeline: USGS, Mount St. Helens - From the 1980 Eruption to 2000; Doukas (1990), USGS Bulletin 1859.
- Hazard speeds: Myers and Brantley (1995), USGS Open-File Report 95-231; USGS Fact Sheet 002-97. Mount Rainier lahars: USGS Cascades Volcano Observatory.
- VEI: Newhall and Self (1982); USGS Volcano Hazards Program; National Park Service (Yellowstone Huckleberry Ridge volume).

The magma viscosities, slopes and the St. Helens cross section are simplified sketches chosen to show the trends, and the panels say so.
